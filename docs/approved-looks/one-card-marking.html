Reads instantly and reads calmly. Colour all round, border included, at a strength you could look at all day. One rule rather than a special case.

How light is light. Measured, not eyeballed: the client's name sits at 17× contrast on the pink and 17× on plain white. No difference at all to read, and the avatars get a ring the colour of the card they sit on so they still cut out cleanly.

What you should be able to do with your eyes, not your head. Count the pink. On seventy deals there are a handful, and they are the only thing in the whole book that is actually late. Everything purple is out of our hands — sitting with a lender or a client — so the morning question "what is actually mine today" has a visual answer for the first time.

Most of the board is still plain white, which is the whole reason the colours work. If half the cards were tinted it would be wallpaper.

Settled is almost entirely green, which is the one column where that is a pleasure rather than information.

And to be clearNothing about how it works changes

This is a repaint. Every behaviour on that board is the behaviour you already have:

The sideways scroll — the pane is the height of what is left of the screen, the bar sits at the foot of it where it is always reachable, and shift with a mouse wheel moves it sideways. Unchanged.

Each column scrolls its own cards while its name, count and total stay pinned. Unchanged — that is Robin's fix from 30 September and I am not touching it.

Dragging a card does exactly what the button on the deal does, and dragging near either edge pulls the next column into view. Unchanged.

Folding a column leaves the count on the strip, because a fold that hides its count is a way to lose deals. Unchanged.

The two lanes in BC, the filters, the counts reading "3 of 6" while a filter is on, the delete under the hover, the quick-look button, the move-back warning. All unchanged.

What actually changes in the code is 37 hand-typed colours becoming about ten named ones, plus one new rule that says which cards get a wash. No layout, no data, no drag, no scroll.

One answer left and I can start. Which surface — White, Paper, Light slate or Slate? Switch between them at the top with the full board on screen; that is the fairest test of the four.

Then the first ship is the invisible one: the colours become names, nothing changes on screen, and a test proves nothing changed. The look goes on in the ship after that, and comes off again in one command if it is wrong in daylight.

Mock-up only. No code has changed. Every name and figure on that board is invented.
