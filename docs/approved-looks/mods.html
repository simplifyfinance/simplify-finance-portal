Checked, not rememberedWhat each tab's sections actually are

An earlier version of this mock put Compliance's five sections under Fact Find. Fabio caught it. So here is the real list, read out of each form file — every tab keeps its own sections, in its own order, with its own count. The only thing that changes is that all five stop being drawn four different ways.

TabIts sections, today and afterHow it is drawn today

What changes is only the third column. Four styles become the one grey pill-bar. The Fact Find bar stops wrapping, which is why Liabilities is currently stranded on a second row by itself.

Your other questionWhich column changes, and which does not

ColumnChanges?What is in it
Far left — the portal sidebarNever Dashboard, Deals, Pipeline and the rest. Identical on every page of the portal, not just this one. Untouched by any of this.
Middle — the workEvery time This is the only part that changes. Pick a different tab and the whole panel changes — Fact Find's fields, then BC's, then Compliance's. Pick a different section inside the tab and the content under the pill-bar changes. This is where your team does the typing.
Right — the railNever The same on all five tabs. Internal notes, the document counts, the three PDF builders, the links out. That is exactly why they can move there: they already sit ABOVE the tabs today precisely because they are not tab-specific. "The same notes on every tab of this deal" is what the portal already says about them.

And that is the whole reason the rail is safe. Nothing is being made tab-specific that was not, and nothing that was tab-specific is being pulled out of its tab. The four strips move sideways, not inwards.

What I proposed and took back outThe six numbers across the top

An earlier version of this had a row of tiles — loan, funds to complete, land value, construction, LVR, documents. They are gone, and they were wrong for a reason worth writing down.

Every one of those figures is already in Deal Structure, which the portal already has and which already holds more than the tiles did: lender, security address, approval type, property value, LVR, land value, construction cost, stamp duty, loan, and every split with its term and product. It works out funds to complete itself.

And it has a rule a tile could never have. From the top of that file: "read-only wherever the value exists somewhere else — the only editable fields are the ones the portal has never recorded anywhere." A tile can only ever be a copy of a number that lives somewhere else, which is the fault this portal has spent a month removing.

Documents was the worst of them. It already appears in the prompt line with a button on it, and again in the rail with all three counts. A tile would have been a third home for one fact.

Deal Structure itself is untouched by any of this. Same block, same place, same fields, same behaviour. Nothing in these four ships goes near it.

Instead of asking you to trust a drawingA test that makes losing a field impossible

A mock-up of a form with 221 fields in it will always be missing something, and checking it by eye is asking you to do a machine's job. So before any rearranging happens, every word a person can read on the five forms is locked — read out of the code, not typed from memory.

831 words, across six files. Labels, placeholders, every option in every dropdown, every button, every section heading. Any ship that drops one fails and names it. Adding is free and silent; removing has to be done by hand in a snapshot file, where it shows up in the diff and in the ship output.

It has already been broken on purpose twice and caught both times — once by shortening “Rent amount” to “Rent”, once by renaming the “Purpose and goals” heading. It reported exactly what went missing, from which form.

And the extractor itself is tested, because a snapshot passes beautifully when the thing doing the reading has quietly stopped reading. Three labels you named by hand were invisible to the first version — they live inside a JSX expression rather than between two tags — and one pattern was so greedy it swallowed the quote that ended the string, hiding the very label it was meant to find.
